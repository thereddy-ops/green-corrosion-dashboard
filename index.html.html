About This Analytics Dashboard

An integrated computational chemistry and computer vision toolkit designed to evaluate how eco-friendly plant extracts protect mild steel coupons from acid-induced corrosion in industrial pickling environments (1.0 M HCl / 0.5 M H₂SO₄).

Traditional toxic chromate and synthetic inhibitors pose severe environmental hazards. Phytochemical extracts from plants such as Mukia maderaspatana, Cinnamomum tamala, and Artemisia herba-alba contain heteroatoms (N, O, S) and aromatic π-electrons that adsorb onto steel surfaces to form a protective barrier. Use the three interactive modules below—or switch via the top navigation bar—to inspect surface degradation, compute mass-loss kinetics, and classify thermodynamic adsorption mechanisms.

MODULE 01
CV Surface Inspector

Uses HSV color-space pixel segmentation to quantify iron oxide rust (Fe₂O₃/FeOOH) and localized pitting cavities. Select a lab coupon preset or upload your own steel photograph, then tune sensitivity sliders to estimate surface coverage (θ) and AFM roughness (Rₐ).

MODULE 02
Gravimetric & Kinetic Calculator

Automates standard mass-loss equations for mild steel (D = 7.86 g/cm³). Load benchmark plant extract presets or input custom pre/post-immersion weights, area, and exposure time to compute corrosion rate (CR), inhibition efficiency (IE%), and Langmuir ratio (C/θ).

MODULE 03
Thermodynamic ΔG°ads Simulator

Evaluates standard free energy of adsorption (ΔG°ads) from equilibrium constant (Kads) and temperature (T). Adjust both sliders to visualize on a real-time regime gauge whether adsorption proceeds via physisorption, chemisorption, or a hybrid mechanism.
